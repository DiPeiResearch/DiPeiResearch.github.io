# Teaching & Mentoring

My teaching philosophy centers on inclusive, student-centered learning environments that combine clear structure with meaningful practice. I help students build foundations in the concepts, theories, and mechanisms that explain how social, behavioral, and communication factors shape health, then apply those foundations to consequential public health decisions.

## Teaching approach

I organize modules around explicit learning objectives and use consistent weekly structures, checklists, examples, rubrics, brief quizzes, and staged assignments to make expectations visible. Applied work asks students to translate theory and evidence into needs assessments, logic models, intervention and message strategies, and implementation and evaluation plans. Examples reflect varied cultural, socioeconomic, and health experiences, and students can participate through discussion, short written responses, collaborative analysis, and independent exercises.

Mentoring includes research ethics, study protocols, recruitment, data collection, instrument and protocol development, qualitative coding, data management, analysis, and dissemination. I begin by translating each student's learning goals into responsibilities and milestones, model how to complete and document research tasks, and provide increasing independence as skills develop.

## Mentoring commitments

I aim to create structured, supportive learning environments in which trainees connect theory to method, gain confidence working with data, and develop accurate, useful writing. Clear structure helps students know where to begin, applied work connects ideas to practice, and increasing independence prepares them for their next academic or professional step.
